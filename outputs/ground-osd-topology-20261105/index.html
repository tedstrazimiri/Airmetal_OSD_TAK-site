Ground-side TAK OSD, wired around a Pi 4B

A connector-level prototype topology for the ORQA GU QS–TAC MATRIX link. Documentary pin numbers are fixed; proposed receiver, driver and bypass hardware remain conditional on measurements.

Pi 4B: suitable for the gateway prototype. Use Linux for TAK, Ghost decoding, MGRS and bounded display updates. AM_osd keeps analog video timing. This architecture avoids a video capture/encode/decode round trip through the Pi.

What the wiring still needs to prove

  • MATRIX 3.3 V peak-to-peak does not establish RS-485 compliance. Confirm common mode, idle polarity and tap loading before connecting U1. Leave existing receiving-end termination intact.
  • Measure video swing, common mode and equalization before designing V1/V2. AD8130/AD8132 are candidate analog building blocks, not plug-compatible ORQA accessories.
  • The four-pole bypass disconnects the active input termination and driver. AM_osd's onboard bypass alone does not preserve video on loss of local power.
  • The gateway still needs verified aircraft azimuth and source timestamps. GPS ground course cannot accept heading-up SA. The existing airborne OSD must be coordinated to reproduce the clean SA page.
  • Enable a stable Pi UART on physical 8/10, disable the serial login console, and reserve it for AM_osd. Avoid boot/login output or a second serial writer on that link.
  • First bench gate: known PAL source → AM_osd → monitor, powered independently, with Pi-generated AM GROUND text. Then characterize MATRIX. No hardware was connected for this figure.
